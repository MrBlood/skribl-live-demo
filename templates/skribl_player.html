How it works
Everything you can do in Skribl Pad, grouped by area. Tap a section to open it.
1
Draw your canvas
Pick a color and brush size and sketch anything. Your first stroke on a blank canvas starts recording automatically, so you can just draw — or hit Record first if you prefer.
2
Record the replay
Hit Record — or, on a blank canvas, just start drawing and recording arms itself. Every stroke is captured with its timing, and Undo / Redo both work mid-take. Hit Stop when you're done — and you don't have to get it in one go: hit Record again to add another take, and the replay plays them all back to back.
3
Add music (optional)
Open the Music tab, add a track, and drag the loop markers to pick up to 20 seconds. Preview Loop plays your selection on repeat, Test Seam checks the join, and Crossfade can smooth it.
4
Add a background photo (optional)
Open the Photo tab to place an image behind your drawing, then frame it with Fill / Fit / Stretch, reposition, zoom, opacity, and blur. Clearing the canvas never removes the photo.
5
Preview and post
Hit Play to watch the replay with your music underneath. When it looks right, tap Post to give it a title and caption — your Skribl is saved on the server and the player link works for anyone you share it with, on any device. If the server can't be reached it's kept on this device only, and the app says so instead of claiming it posted.
Pen
Your main drawing tool. Picking any color automatically switches you back to the Pen, so you never erase by accident.
Eraser
Removes parts of your drawing. A circle follows your cursor showing the exact area it clears — the eraser is 3× your current brush size, so it covers more with each pass.
Brush size
The four circles show the actual dot size you'll draw with; the active one highlights in purple. The eraser scales with it.
More tools
The tool row stays minimal — tap More tools for color, background, opacity, and smoothing. The colored dot on the button shows your current pen color at a glance. Tap again to collapse.
Color
Tap any swatch to draw with it, or the + dot to mix a custom color. Your recent custom colors appear in a Recent row (kept between sessions) for quick reuse.
Background
The Background swatches change the color behind your whole drawing — not the strokes themselves — and the eraser reveals it. Tap + for a custom background color.
Opacity
Lowers how solid each new stroke is, for translucent layering. While you're drawing, a translucent stroke stays one even layer — crossing over your own line won't darken it — and it sets in place when you lift. Set opacity before you draw: it bakes into the stroke.
Smoothing
Off / Low / High steadies shaky lines as you draw — especially handy on a phone. It only affects new strokes, never ones already drawn.
Eyedropper
Pick a color from anywhere on screen (or, in some browsers, tap the canvas) and it becomes your pen color.
Undo / Redo
Step back and forward stroke by stroke, up to 30 steps. Both work while recording — the take captures the drawing as corrected. Redo history clears the moment you draw something new.
Auto-record
On a blank, unlocked canvas your first stroke starts recording by itself — so if you just start drawing, you still end up with a replay to post. Prefer to be deliberate? Hitting Record first does exactly the same thing.
Two timers
While recording you'll see clock time and play time. Long pauses between strokes are shortened on playback, so play time — how long the finished replay actually runs — can be much shorter than the clock. Play time accumulates across all your takes.
Canvas locks between takes
When you hit Stop, the canvas locks so the captured replay can't be drawn over by accident. It's not final, though — recording again unlocks it and adds to the same Skribl.
Add more takes
Hit Record on a locked canvas and a new take is added to the same Skribl, picking up right where the drawing left off — earlier takes aren't replaced or frozen. The finished replay plays every take back to back, in order, so you can build a Skribl in as many sittings as you like. Clear is the only way to start over from blank.
Preview & stop
Play replays your take with the music loop underneath; while it runs the button becomes Stop, which ends the preview early.
Add a track
Tap the Music dropzone to browse, or drag an audio file straight onto it. MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, and AAC all work.
Loop markers
Drag the two handles on the waveform to choose the section that plays under your drawing — up to 20 seconds. The selected range highlights in purple, and the loop repeats for as long as the replay runs.
Move the loop
Grab the middle of the purple selection and slide the whole loop to another part of the song, keeping its length — an easy way to audition the same duration over different sections.
Loop Detail
Zooms in on your selection so you can place the loop points precisely, with a moving playhead while you preview. Focus buttons (Loop / Start / End) center the view, and the magnifier (1×–8×) zooms right up to a boundary.
Scroll
The Scroll slider (or dragging the zoomed waveform) pans the Loop Detail window anywhere along the song, so you can inspect any part of the track at full zoom.
Match Drawing
Snaps the loop length to how long your drawing replay runs, so the music and the drawing finish together. It resizes in place, keeping your current start point.
Fine-tune
The Start and End − / + buttons nudge each loop edge, with the current time shown between them. The Step control sets how far each nudge moves — down to a hundredth of a second. Press and hold to repeat.
Crossfade
A plain loop is a hard cut from end back to start — if those two points don't match you hear a click on every wrap. Crossfade blends the loop's tail into its head so the wrap becomes seamless. It's baked into the loop you preview, post, and export; it never alters your original track. Default is off.
Preview Loop
Plays your selected section on repeat — exactly what will loop under the drawing, crossfade included.
Test Seam
Plays just the last moment of your loop and then wraps to the start, so you can hear whether the join is smooth before you post. If it clicks, nudge the loop points or add some Crossfade.
Add a photo
Tap the Photo dropzone to browse, or drag an image onto it. JPG, PNG, GIF, and WebP are supported. The photo sits behind your drawing on its own layer — strokes and the eraser never touch it.
Fill / Fit / Stretch
Fill covers the whole canvas (cropping if needed), Fit shows the entire image inside it, and Stretch forces it to the exact canvas shape.
Reposition
In Fill, part of your photo is cropped. Tap Reposition photo, then drag the image to choose which part shows behind your drawing — the drag moves the photo, never draws.
Zoom
Also in Fill, the Zoom slider enlarges the photo inside the canvas — then reposition to frame exactly the part you want.
Opacity
Fades the photo back so your drawing stands out on top of it.
Blur
Softens the photo so it reads as a backdrop and your strokes pop. Slide back to zero for a sharp image.
Reset adjustments
Puts fit, opacity, blur, position, and zoom back to their defaults without removing the photo.
More menu
The ··· button in the top bar holds Save draft, Load draft, Export, and Clear canvas.
Autosave
About a second after you stop editing, your work is saved on this device automatically — a small Saved status confirms it. It protects against closed tabs and crashes; you never have to trigger it.
Restore on reload
Reopen the editor after a reload and a banner offers your last session back, with when it was saved and what it contains. Restore brings it back; Discard starts fresh.
Re-adding photo & music
Autosave keeps your drawing plus every setting — loop points, crossfade, photo framing, filenames — but not the photo and music files themselves (browser storage is too small for them). The restore banner lists what to re-add; drop the same files back in and your saved settings, including the loop trim, reapply automatically. To save everything in one piece, use Save draft.
Save draft
Saves the complete Skribl — drawing, replay, background, photo, and music with its loop points — as a .skribl file on your device that you can reopen and keep editing later. Nothing needs re-adding.
Load draft
Reopens a saved .skribl file, restoring everything exactly as you left it. A loaded replay opens locked, just like a finished recording — hit Record to add another take on top of it.
Export
Save your Skribl as a PNG image of the finished drawing, or a WebM video of the stroke replay with your looped music — for sharing outside the app.
Clear canvas
Wipes the drawing and its recording so you can start over. Your background photo stays put. This can't be undone, so save a draft first if you might want it back.
When you Post, your Skribl is saved on the server and the player link works for anyone, on any device. If the server can't be reached, posting falls back to saving on this device only — and the app says “Saved on this device only” instead of claiming it posted.
Post
Opens a sheet to give your Skribl a title and an optional caption, with a preview of the drawing. Posting saves it to the server and hands back a player link you can share. Shared links unfurl with your title, caption, and a picture of the drawing.
Watch your Skribl
After posting, tap Watch your Skribl to open it in the player — the same replay, presented on its own with no editing tools.
Player controls
Play starts the replay and becomes Pause, which freezes it in place; tap again to carry on. Restart plays from the beginning, and the loop toggle replays it automatically. If the Skribl has music, a mute button silences it.
Scrub bar
Drag or tap the progress bar to jump to any moment — the drawing redraws to exactly that instant, mid-stroke and all. Scrubbing pauses playback; when you resume, the music picks up in step with the drawing.
Copy link
Copies the player link to share with anyone — it opens the posted Skribl for them too. (For a device-only fallback save, the link only opens on this device.)
