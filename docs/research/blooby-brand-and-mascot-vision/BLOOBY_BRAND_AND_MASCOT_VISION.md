# Blooby — Brand and Mascot Vision

*Creative concept exploration — not a proposed redesign or an approved implementation plan.*

I've actually developed a stronger opinion about Blooby after everything we've discussed.

**I think Blooby should be more than a mascot for a drawing website. He should be a drawing that somehow came to life.**

And I think that one idea could tie together your brand, Skribl's replay technology, Draw back, and the entire social experience.

Not through a redesign. Through personality, behavior, and a little storytelling.

## My favorite concept: Blooby is the first living Skribl

> **“Somebody drew him. Then he got up.”**

Imagine Blooby's little origin story: somebody draws a purple jelly bean with a face and two black boots.

The drawing finishes.

A moment later, one eye blinks.

Then the other.

His little boots move.

He looks around, realizes somebody is watching, and smiles.

**He isn't a mascot pretending to be a drawing. He actually is one.**

This gives his imperfect, handmade appearance a reason to exist. It also makes Skribl's technology part of the brand's personality.

And because he came from a drawing, he's naturally curious about other drawings.

He peeks at them, watches them develop, sometimes adds something, and gets excited when another artist draws back.

That's a character with a purpose.

## Five ideas I'd explore

### 1. Blooby is always peeking into creativity.

Remember that version of him peeking over an edge that you liked? I'd make it his signature gesture. He peeks over canvases, out from behind drawings, around empty panels, and from the edge of the Gallery. He's curious about what people are making, never interrupting it.

### 2. Blooby has a family tree of his own.

His original drawing becomes the beginning of a collaborative drawing family. People can Draw back on him: make him a friend, place him in an underwater world, give him a birthday party, or create an animated adventure. The official character stays the same, but the community builds his world.

This would demonstrate Blooby's most distinctive social feature through the mascot himself.

### 3. Blooby never simply appears—he draws himself.

In a few special moments, his outline could emerge stroke by stroke, his purple color is sketched in, and his little feet appear last. Then he walks away. It would be a recognizable signature animation and a demonstration of what Skribl does.

### 4. Give him personality without giving him a lot of dialogue.

I picture him as curious, slightly shy, a little mischievous, and easily delighted. His pink cheek, eyes, and tiny boots do almost all the communicating.

When something wonderful happens, he doesn't announce it in a giant speech bubble. He does a little happy hop. When a canvas is empty, he peeks over its edge as if wondering what might appear.

### 5. Blooby should never be a perfect artist.

This is important. I wouldn't portray him as a brilliant illustrator showing everyone how to draw.

I'd make him an enthusiastic doodler whose drawings are sometimes wonderfully terrible.

His appeal is that he doesn't care whether a drawing is professional. He cares that somebody made it.

## What I think the brand itself should stand for

I wouldn't position Blooby as an art program for serious artists, nor as a children's drawing site.

I'd position it as a **creative social playground** that welcomes every skill level.

The feeling should be that you don't need talent, equipment, or a reason to draw. You can simply make something, watch it come alive, and see what happens when somebody else adds to it.

And I think the names already work together:

- **blooby.** — The place, the community, and the little character who welcomes you.
- **skribl** — The creative medium. Draw it in Pad or animate it in Flip.
- **draw back** — The uniquely social act of adding your own creation to someone else's.

Notice how those three ideas almost describe an experience without additional explanation.

You go to Blooby. You make a Skribl. Someone draws back.

## A few brand lines I like

1. **“Make a mark. See where it goes.”** — My favorite. It captures creativity, replay, and collaboration without sounding technical.
2. **“Draw it. Watch it. Pass it on.”** — The clearest explanation of the whole experience.
3. **“Little drawings. Big possibilities.”** — Friendly and inviting, especially for beginners.
4. **“Every drawing has a story.”** — Emphasizes timed replay, history, and family trees.
5. **“A little doodle can go a long way.”** — More whimsical and especially suited to Blooby's personality.

## One more idea I particularly like

Imagine Blooby is the first character on the site who gets a Draw back.

His original drawing is extremely simple. Just the familiar purple shape, face, cheek, and boots.

Someone draws a flower beside him.

Somebody else draws him holding it.

Another artist makes the flower grow in Flip.

Another draws a garden.

Eventually, there are hundreds of different branches, and people can follow how a simple little drawing turned into a community creation.

**Blooby becomes a living example of what the entire platform is about.**

And importantly, nobody needs to change the original Blooby. He's still the same slightly imperfect purple jelly bean.

That's part of the charm.

## What I would absolutely not change

I wouldn't give Blooby fancy clothes, oversized cartoon eyes, a glossy 3D body, or a modern animated-movie appearance.

I wouldn't make him an AI assistant that pops up constantly.

And I wouldn't make everything about him. The people's creations should always be more important than the mascot.

**I'd keep the original Blooby exactly as recognizable as he is.** His unusual jelly-bean shape, his one pink cheek, the tiny black boots, and even the handmade irregularities are the point.

What makes him memorable shouldn't be how elaborately he's rendered. It should be what he does.

## My favorite overall direction

**Blooby is a little drawing who came to life and discovered that other drawings could come to life, too.**

He's fascinated by them.

He wants to see what people make.

And when one person adds to another person's creation, he's delighted—because that's how his world grows.

There's a gentle emotional quality to that which I think suits your project: every little creation matters, even the imperfect ones.

If I could choose one phrase to guide the brand, it would be:

> **“Make a mark. See where it goes.”**

It works for a single stroke, a replay, a Draw back, a family tree, and an entire creative community.

And the mascot who represents that idea is already sitting in your project. He doesn't need to look different.

**He just needs to feel alive.**
